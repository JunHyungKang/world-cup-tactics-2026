## 1. The manager's dilemma comes first

Headline: `네 구역 중 두 곳만. 결과를 보기 전에 감독이 먼저 정합니다.`
The manager has only two attention tokens for the next set-piece meeting.
Placing them on the pitch makes two priorities explicit and leaves the other
two areas outside this meeting's priority review. Only after that choice and a
minimum overlap criterion are locked does the evidence appear.

The fixed campaign uses 48 group-stage reference matches, eight round-of-16
evaluation matches, and eight sealed quarter-final-and-later verification
matches. The 48→8→8 structure supports the manager's dilemma; it is not the
opening experience. Corner Policy Lab is not an answer engine. It gives the
manager a scarce choice, preserves it, and lets unseen records argue back.

## 2. A real manager commitment

The manager places two priorities directly on the pitch or through accessible
cards, declares a minimum location-overlap criterion of 40%, 50%, or 60%, may
abstain, and locks before any held-out match identity or outcome appears. One immutable fingerprint
binds the areas and criterion in both sequential tests.
The score is delivery-location overlap only, never defensive effectiveness.

Locking freezes the selected area IDs, minimum criterion, abstention state, split rule, and policy
fingerprint. The area controls then stay disabled. The manager can inspect the
first result and its contradiction, but cannot silently tune the policy before
opening the final eight matches. After the sealed result, the manager records a
separate next-meeting note: keep the priorities, revise them at the next
meeting, or defer judgment. This note never rewrites the sealed policy, overlap
criterion verdicts, results, or evaluation receipts.

## 3. Evidence argues back

Every classifiable corner in the revealed partition appears in the record table.
A deterministic representative contradiction is the first unselected-area shot,
otherwise the first unselected delivery, otherwise a selected-area shot. It is
representative, not statistically strongest. The match, event ID, action,
outcome, source, and forbidden inference remain visible. Each held-out result
also closes as `사전 기준 충족` or `사전 기준 미달`, using only the criterion
declared before exposure.

## 5. Why this is not another tactics dashboard

A statistics dashboard lets the manager inspect what already happened. An AI
recommendation asks the manager to follow a score. A generic tactics board
stops after placement. Corner Policy Lab instead asks for a choice before the
answer is visible, binds the manager's own criterion, shows a held-out
contradiction, and closes with a separate next-meeting decision.

The ontology remains a compact safety mechanism rather than the product hero.
The policy fingerprint is computed only from the sorted selected-area IDs,
abstention state, minimum overlap criterion, and fixed 48/8/8 split rule.
Separately, each result receipt exposes the inspectable evidence path
`MatchContext → CornerRestart → DeliveryAction → ObservedEvent → Source`,
including the source relation `DERIVED_FROM`. The interface forbids
`DEFENSIVE_DUTY_CAUSED`, `WOULD_PREVENT`, and `OPTIMAL_POLICY`. It does not
generate missing actions, counterfactual effects, or causal recommendations.

## 6. Actual 60-second proof

The judge selects two priorities, declares one minimum overlap criterion, and
locks one immutable policy snapshot before
exposure. A second action reveals all eight round-of-16 evaluations and eight
receipts with the same fingerprint while the final eight matches stay sealed. A
third action applies that identical snapshot to the sealed final verification and
opens its representative contradiction. The manager then chooses one next-meeting response
and leaves a short reason, completing the decision loop
without changing the experiment that just finished.

The refreshed local static-release rehearsal must fit the two priority choices,
one predeclared criterion, one policy lock, two reveals, one next-meeting
response, and one saved note into
60 seconds with burned Korean captions. It remains local until the exact public
release is recorded and uploaded; it is not human evidence.

## 7. One meeting, three decisions, one public proof

The target user is a manager or analyst preparing a set-piece meeting. The
practical problem is not “find every pattern”; it is “which two areas deserve
the limited attention of this meeting, and what evidence would make us revisit
that choice?”

Stage 1 is commitment: place two attention tokens, choose a 40%, 50%, or 60%
minimum overlap criterion, and lock. Stage 2 is confrontation: open the
round-of-16 evidence, inspect the same-fingerprint receipts and representative
contradiction, then apply the unchanged policy to the sealed final eight.
Stage 3 is the next decision: keep, revise at the next meeting, or defer, with a
short reason that cannot rewrite the completed test.

The stamped keyless public release is live without installation, login, or an
API key. BG-01–15 pass 60/60 across Chromium, Firefox, WebKit, and mobile,
including invalid-data fail-closed behavior and the full next-meeting path.
The final video candidate is bound to the same release. Product selection is
`PASS`; causal recommendation is `REJECT`; the empirical campaign is `REVISE`;
human evidence remains `unavailable/no-claim`.
